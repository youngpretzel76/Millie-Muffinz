Welcome to the Garden of Grief.

Waves

1 am, I woke up to crashing in my room near my door. I saw Millie having a seizure. She'd never have one before. I got upset, obviously, after knowing that my baby hadn't been doing well since that Monday.

I ran to be by her side and I was reassuring her as she was convulsing. I began to cry. I didn't know what to do. I was extremely sensitive towards seizures in animals.

After she let up, I put her on the bed with me where she growled and did not move until she had her second seizure. She never growled. As I've said before, she was the sweetest little lady ever.

Seizures make animals violent, as they've been hurting, and are confused, disoriented, and stressed. I felt nothing but worry for her.

I knew in that moment that she was not going to last until the end of the week. I had to come to terms with the fact that Millie, my baby, my DAUGHTER, was dying before my eyes.

While she lasted for a while, she was slowly declining. I didn't want her to suffer any longer, so I had to decide what to do. No one was awake, so I stayed awake until it turned 5 am. Time for me to wake up.

She had another seizure around this time, and I immediately texted my parents after she let up again. They knew what that meant, and my mom asked me if I wanted to help her cross the rainbow bridge that morning.

I told her that I had to. I didn't want to. Part of me wanted to be selfish and keep Millie alive so I'd have more time with her. But I would never forgive myself.

Had I done that, and she died on my watch? I don't think I'd ever be the same. That's why I made the choice I made that morning. It was difficult. I was wondering if I made the right one, if I was even a good cat dad.

I was terrified. This was my life, my child, my world. I had to give her up so her energy would return to the balance that keeps our planet going.

I agreed to have my mother call into the vet when it opened. She had another seizure around the time they opened, 7:30 am. After that, she hadn't seized. I thought she would be okay, but I knew that I couldn't sustain her any longer.

Even if her seizures would've stopped, would she really be alive?

We put her in a blanket, decorated in stars and rocket ships. My room is space themed, as was her bed. I thought it'd be right, for her to explore space one last time.

She started her fourth convulsion in the car. That ended up getting us in quicker than it would've had she not seized. She was drooling everywhere. I felt so bad, I couldn't hold her.

We went to exam room 3. She laid on the table. She was too small to put a catheter in, so they gave her a sedative. Her big eyes still laid open as she was sleeping. I just hope that helped her ease.

Then, after choosing where I wanted her to remain, they gave her the shot. She laid peacefully. I held her until she stopped breathing. I was sobbing hysterically. My world for 4 years, gone like that. It's crazy how life can end in an instant. How everything can change within a second.

It's been a difficult set of weeks. A whole month of her being gone. It's still surreal. Sometimes I hear her in my room. I couldn't lay in my room for a week. I refused to.

To this day, it's still hard to speak about. I wish she were here with me again. My difficulties would be so much more easier to manage. But, that's how life works.

It's not permanent. Soon, we all have to return to nature.

The Little Things

She's still here with me. Her hair, her pawprint, her ashes. Her sounds. Her scent. Her bed. Everything remains. Yet time doesn't.

Time is running out. Before I know it, it will have been a year. 5 years. 10. The rest of my life will pass by and will I still think of her then? One could only hope.

5:00 pm. The time I used to feed her. Every day. A routine. A routine I loved doing, seeing her perk up at the sound of the can opening. Her eating. The messes she'd make.

Does it mean anything now? Does the same time really hold the same meaning as it once did? Will I be able to keep this up? How will I fill that empty slot?

Do I cry? Do I smile? Do I sit there and think about everything, and how I can't do this without her? How do I keep going? How do I sleep at night knowing she's never coming back?

Perhaps being so sentimental about it makes it worse. But not talking about it would as well. It's been leaving me at a crossroads. How do I do anything?

I'll figure it out soon enough. Maybe I won't, maybe I'm not meant to. Life has its ways. Confusing, yet comforting ways. Maybe, just maybe, I'll have an answer then.
